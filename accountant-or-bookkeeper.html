Accountant or bookkeeper: which does your business need?

A bookkeeper records the day-to-day transactions of a business. An accountant turns those records into year-end accounts and tax returns, and advises on tax and structure. Many small businesses need both jobs done, but not always by two people.

What each one does

Recording sales, purchases and receiptsBookkeeper
Bank reconciliationBookkeeper
Preparing VAT returnsEither
Running payrollEither
Year-end statutory accountsAccountant
Corporation tax return (CT600)Accountant
Self Assessment with business or rental incomeAccountant
Tax planning, salary and dividendsAccountant
Dealing with HMRC enquiriesAccountant

When you need an accountant

  • You run a limited company. It must file statutory accounts and a CT600 each year.
  • Your turnover is close to the £90,000 VAT registration threshold.
  • You are deciding between sole trader and limited company.
  • You have income from more than one source, such as a job, a business and property.
  • You have had a letter, penalty or enquiry from HMRC.
  • You need accounts or a reference for a mortgage, visa or lender.

What "Chartered" means

Anyone in the UK can call themselves an accountant. A Chartered Accountant has passed professional exams, completed supervised training and is a member of a body such as ICAEW or ACCA. A firm in practice must hold a practising certificate, carry professional indemnity insurance and be supervised for anti-money laundering.

How to check an accountant

  • Search the ICAEW "Find a Chartered Accountant" directory or the ACCA member directory for the person or firm.
  • Ask which body supervises the firm for anti-money laundering.
  • Ask for the fee in writing before work starts, and what it covers.

Questions

Can a bookkeeper file my company accounts?

A bookkeeper can prepare the records, but statutory accounts and the corporation tax return are normally prepared by an accountant, who takes responsibility for the tax treatment.

Do I need an accountant as a sole trader?

It is not a legal requirement. Many sole traders use one for the tax return, to claim the right expenses and to prepare for Making Tax Digital.

Is an online accountant as good as a local one?

The qualifications and regulation are the same. What matters is who prepares and reviews your work and how quickly they reply.
